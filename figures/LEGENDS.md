# SPECTRA figure legends

Primary endpoint throughout: locked six-layer SPECTRA ensemble (three seeds, equal probability mean), one predefined primary sample group per patient, held-out test cohort of 713 cancer-cohort patients and 44 non-cancer controls (NBD, EPIC, INTC17-C, PANC-Ctrl). AUROC 95% confidence intervals are stratified patient bootstrap percentiles (4,000 replicates); sensitivity and specificity intervals are Wilson intervals at the fixed reported cut-offs (Supplementary Table, confidence_intervals.csv). Operating points are ROC-derived at nominal 90% and 95% specificity (achieved 40/44 and 42/44); no threshold calibration.

**Figure 1 | Enrichment-free whole-slide cellular liquid biopsy and the SPECTRA model.**
**a**, Workflow (vector redrawing of the authors' BioRender panel). Sample preparation: 1.5 mL peripheral blood is centrifuged and all nucleated cells are plated without CTC enrichment as a monolayer across the whole slide. Two slides are analysed per patient (two for 2,467 of 2,550 patients; drawn one behind the other), each stained for DAPI, cytokeratin (CK), vimentin (VIM) and CD45/CD31, and imaged across the whole slide (fWSI); every cell is segmented and embedded as a 512-dimensional vector by a frozen contrastive encoder trained without labels (BLUE v2; Supplementary Fig. 1a), and anomaly detection keeps the 10,000 most unusual cells per slide (the red tail of the anomaly-score histogram). Bar: each slide, with its roughly 2.5 million nucleated cells sorted by anomaly score; almost all are ordinary leukocytes (grey; proportions illustrative) and the rare-event candidates (red) are fewer than 0.5% of cells, drawn wider than to scale so that they are visible. The red line carries each patient's rare events, from both slides, to the model. **b**, SPECTRA. Each rare event that passes image-quality, leukocyte and fluorescence-intensity filters (Supplementary Fig. 1) is one token; a patient contributes the cells of up to two slides. A learnable summary token (CLS) starts empty; in each of six transformer blocks (four attention heads each) it attends to every cell, so after the last block it holds a patient summary that one output layer turns into P(cancer). Training is supervised only by patient diagnosis. The SPECTRA score is P(cancer) averaged over three independently trained models. Attribution (AttnLRP) traces the score back to individual cells; cell frames are shaded by attribution. Cells are schematic drawings. **c**, Primary cohort by cancer type (dark, training, 1,793 patients; light, held-out test, 757). Myeloma includes related plasma-cell states. Controls with confirmed benign lung nodules (81) and BRCA-mutation carriers without breast cancer (22) are held out of general training (Fig. 5).

**Figure 2 | Detection on the held-out test cohort.**
**a**, Pooled ROC, 713 cancers versus 44 non-cancer controls; AUROC 0.82 (95% CI 0.75–0.89), macro AUROC over the eight types with ≥10 cases 0.82 (95% CI 0.74–0.89). Sensitivity 56.4% (95% CI 52.7–60.0) at 90.9% specificity (95% CI 78.8–96.4) and 39.6% (95% CI 36.0–43.2) at 95.5% specificity (95% CI 84.9–98.7). **b**, ROC for each cancer type with ≥10 test cases, each against the same 44 controls (AUROC in legend, 95% CIs in the Supplementary Table; *myeloma includes related plasma-cell states). **c**, SPECTRA score (log-odds of the ensemble probability) for every one of the 757 test patients by cancer type, including rare types; dark points are called at the 90% specificity cut-off, vertical bars are medians, dashed lines mark the 90% and 95% specificity cut-offs (40/44 and 42/44 controls negative). Per-type AUROCs and sensitivities at both cut-offs are listed in the source table (primary_per_cancer_reporting.csv).

**Figure 3 | The cells that drive detection.**
AttnLRP attribution of the locked ensemble on all 757 test patients (inference only). **a**, Atlas of the cells carrying 90% of each patient's positive attribution (204,131 cells; two-dimensional UMAP layout of the frozen embeddings, for display only; numbers mark each cluster's densest region), coloured by Leiden cluster (23 clusters, every cell assigned). Around it, one real cell image per cluster, 40x for the 19 clusters with re-images and 10x (labelled) for the other four (numbered as on the map; visually selected and centred on the matched cell; dim tiles linearly brightened for display; bars, 5 µm). **b**, Atlas table ordered from cancer-enriched (top) to control-enriched (bottom): median marker intensity (dot size, absolute 16-bit scale; names summarize median marker levels, not cell identities); abundance difference between cancers and non-cancer controls among all model-input cells (AUROC of per-patient cluster share; *, BH q<0.05); and model emphasis (log2 of a cluster's share among attributed cells over its share among all cells, in cancers). CK+, CD45/31− cells (20) are more common in cancer and up-weighted, and CD45/31-dim cells (5) are more common in controls and down-weighted, in all three seeds; DAPI-bright small cells (1) are more common in cancer, but their up-weighting is not consistent across seeds (Supplementary Fig. 3). **c**, Cluster composition alone (L2 logistic regression on centred log-ratio cluster shares, within-test 5x5 CV) reaches 0.74 versus SPECTRA 0.82. Other Leiden seeds give 20–22 clusters (ARI 0.56, 0.51). Attribution is not causal and does not establish malignancy; descriptive. Full gallery in Supplementary Fig. 4.

**Figure 4 | SPECTRA versus CTC enumeration: CK-defined CTCs are one part of circulating biology and do not separate cancer from non-cancer, while SPECTRA does not depend on them.**
**a**, ROC on identical patients (713 cancers, 44 non-cancer controls): SPECTRA versus the number of CK-gated cells (CK >40,000 and CD45/CD31 <10,000), automated CTC-like cells and CTCs only per patient (summed over the patient's analysed slides; two slides for 732 of 757 patients). Paired AUROC differences (SPECTRA minus marker): CK-gated 0.32 (95% CI 0.20–0.43), CTC-like 0.34 (95% CI 0.22–0.44), CTCs only 0.36 (95% CI 0.23–0.46). **b**, SPECTRA score by number of CK-gated cells per patient for cancers and non-cancer controls (numbers, patients per group); 197 cancer patients have no CK-gated cell, and their scores remain high. **c**, Breast cancer by stage (69 Unknown, 1 Conflicting not shown) for SPECTRA and for the numbers of CK-gated and CTC-like cells per patient, against the same 44 controls; SPECTRA early 0.82 (95% CI 0.73–0.90), late 0.86 (95% CI 0.78–0.93). **d**, Cell removal on the locked model: sensitivity at the original 90%-specificity cut-off after deleting each cell set from every patient, versus deleting the same number of random cells (mean of 5 draws; dashed, intact model). Removing CK-gated, CK-bright or all CTC-like cells leaves sensitivity intact; removing top-attributed cells collapses it, confirming that attribution identifies the cells the model uses. **e**, What those cells look like when no CK-gated cell is present: four detected cancers without a CK-gated cell and, for each, the six most-attributed cells that have 40x re-images, in attribution order (bars, 5 µm; 40x, 0.18 µm per pixel). Many are large, vimentin-bright cells. Counts are model-input candidate cells, not confirmed tumour cells.

**Figure 5 | Adapting SPECTRA to harder diagnostic questions changes which cells it relies on.**
Lung cancer versus confirmed benign lung nodules in heavy smokers, and breast cancer versus BRCA-mutation carriers without breast cancer, using control populations held out of all general training. **a**, Held-out test AUROC for the unchanged pan-cancer model and after adaptation. Adaptation refits only the decision layer of the frozen model: everything that reads the cells is unchanged, so each patient's 256-number summary is the same, and only the final step from that summary to P(cancer) is refitted, as a logistic regression, on the task's training patients (152 lung cancers and 58 benign-nodule controls; 531 breast cancers and 14 BRCA carriers). Bold, paired gain: lung 0.10 (95% CI −0.04 to 0.24), interval includes zero; breast 0.35 (95% CI 0.10 to 0.62). Test: 62 lung cases and 23 controls; 218 breast cases and 8 controls. Other adaptation methods are compared in Supplementary Fig. 8. **b**, Mean score percentile of cancers and diagnostic controls before and after adaptation (refitted decision layer). Patients are ranked by score within each task's test cohort (0, lowest; 100, highest); percentiles are used because the unchanged model gives almost every patient in these cohorts a probability near 1, so raw scores are not comparable between models. Controls fall while cancers stay put. **c**,**d**, Where the evidence comes from, before and after adaptation (refitted decision layer). For every test patient, each cell's positive attribution is summed by Figure 3 cluster and expressed as a share of that patient's total; bars show the change in share (adapted minus pan-cancer, percentage points), averaged with cancers and diagnostic controls weighted equally, for the six clusters with the largest change that moves in the same direction in both groups (all clusters in the source data); hatched bar, all CK+ clusters combined (lung −2.6, breast +3.1 points: in lung CK+ cluster 21 gains while cluster 20 loses; in breast the gain is through CK+ CD45/31+ clusters 18 and 19). This describes which cells the score is built from, not the size of the score (b). In both tasks large vimentin-high cells (cluster 10) lose weight. **e**,**f**, Individual cells behind these shifts: the test-patient cells whose share of their patient's positive evidence rises (top) or falls (bottom) most after adaptation, among cells with a 40x re-image, one cell per patient, excluding cells whose cluster as a whole moves at least 0.5 points the other way (so a gaining cell is not drawn from a losing cluster). Cells are from cancers and diagnostic controls (no BRCA-carrier cell has a 40x re-image). Cells losing weight are mostly large vimentin-high cells; cells gaining weight include CK+ cells. One fixed 26 µm field centred on the matched cell; bars, 10 µm. BRCA-carrier test controls are few (8), so the breast result is preliminary.

**Supplementary Fig. 1 | From blood slide to model input.** **a**, Training data of the frozen BLUE v2 cell encoder: 691 slides from 507 patients across 10 cancer types and two non-cancer conditions (*), 3.8 million rare-event crops (75 × 75 pixels; DAPI, cytokeratin, CD45/CD31, vimentin and the cell mask) after leukocyte depletion; ResNet-18 trained by contrastive learning (SimCLR) without labels for 100 epochs, giving a 512-number profile per cell. The encoder predates this study's patient split, so some study patients may be among its training slides; it never saw diagnosis labels. **b**, Cells per patient for every primary-analysis patient (2,550; summed over the patient's analysed slides, two for 2,467 patients and one for 83), from about 5 million nucleated cells (about 2.5 million per slide; typical count, not measured) to 10,000 rare-event candidates per slide (20,000 for two slides), those passing the image-quality filter (unusable probability ≤0.465) and those also passing the leukocyte (score <0.9) and fluorescence-intensity (TRITC, FITC or CY5 mean ≥5,000) filters, which SPECTRA reads. **c**, The cells SPECTRA reads per patient, by frozen BLUE v2 ten-class classifier call (CTC-like = imCTC, CTC and pcCTC classes; immune-like = lymphocyte, granulocyte and monocyte classes), and CK-gated cells (CK >40,000 and CD45/CD31 <10,000). Violins, distribution over patients; numbers, medians (medians do not sum to the total). Classifier calls and gates describe phenotypes, not confirmed cell identities.

**Supplementary Fig. 2 | Benchmark and score diagnostics.**
**a**, Matched three-fold, two-seed training-cohort CV (15 epochs, fixed budget); points, folds; bar, mean. Count model: cubic spline on log total model-input events. **b**, Same-fold SPECTRA minus baseline. **c**, Test seed agreement: 86% of single-seed probabilities are <0.02 or >0.98; seeds agree on most cancers but split nearly uniformly on controls. **d**, Score is unrelated to model-input cell number. **e**, Test AUROC by sample-group endpoint; primary group reported. **f**, Acquisition batch check on the test cohort (no refits): AUROC counting only cancer-control pairs scanned on the same scanner (BZ3, BZ4 or BZ5; 9,996 pairs), in the same calendar year, within 180 days of each other, or in the same quartile of DAPI exposure (set automatically by the scanner per slide), versus all pairs (dashed). Scores do not differ by scanner within cancers (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.33) or within controls (p=0.45) and are unrelated to scan date (Spearman ρ 0.00 cancers, 0.25 controls); score remains predictive after adjusting for scanner and scan year (likelihood-ratio p<10⁻¹⁶). Each tube is processed as its own experiment, so processing batch cannot be separated from patient.

**Supplementary Fig. 3 | Attribution sanity checks.** **a**, Cascading model randomization (inference only; 44 non-cancer controls and 76 randomly chosen cancers from the test cohort): layers of each locked model are re-initialized with default initializers from the classifier downward and AttnLRP is recomputed (three-seed mean, as in Fig. 3). Lines show median per-patient agreement with the trained-model map. Re-initializing only the classifier drops the signed rank correlation from 1 to 0.33; attribution magnitude and the top-50 cell set are partly retained (0.52 overlap) until the first transformer block is re-initialized, after which agreement falls to chance (random top-50 overlap 0.03). So which cells count for or against cancer depends on the trained readout, while about half of the most-attributed cells are cells that stand out in the learned early representation. Dashed line, top-50 overlap between independently trained seeds (0.20): single-cell rankings are model-specific, which is why cell-level results are reported as three-seed means and summarised by cluster. **b**, Cluster emphasis (Fig. 3b) recomputed from each seed's own attributed cells against the three-seed value; seed-to-seed rank correlation 0.63–0.77; 15 of 23 clusters have the same direction in all three seeds, including the vimentin-high large (10, 11), CK+ (18–21) and CD45/31 (5, 13) clusters discussed in the text. **c**, How concentrated the evidence is: share of each patient's positive attribution carried by its top-ranked cells (lines, median; bands, interquartile range; 713 cancers, 44 non-cancer controls). In cancers, half of the positive evidence sits in a median of 15 cells (about 1% of the cells read), 75% in 70 and 90% in 220; the 90% set used for the atlas (Fig. 3) is therefore broader than the core evidence. Cluster emphasis keeps its direction at stricter cut-offs (Spearman with the 90% set: top 10 cells 0.77, top 50 0.88, 75% 0.92).

**Supplementary Fig. 5 | Cell embeddings carry diagnostic information within clusters, beyond cluster abundance.** Held-out test cohort (713 cancer patients, 44 non-cancer controls), using all model-input cells of each patient; no attribution or SPECTRA scores enter this analysis. **a**, For each Figure 3 cluster, AUROC for separating cancer patients from controls by two measures. Open grey: the cluster's share of each patient's input cells (abundance; Fig. 3b). Teal: the mean embedding (first 50 principal components of the normalized BLUE v2 embedding) of the patient's cells in that cluster alone (within-cluster embedding; logistic regression, C = 0.1, class-balanced, mean over 5 × 5 repeated stratified cross-validation within the test cohort). Patients with fewer than three cells in a cluster are left out for that cluster (patients per cluster in the source data). Filled teal, label-permutation P < 0.05 (200 permutations); asterisk, Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05. Clusters are ordered by within-cluster embedding AUROC. **b**, The same two AUROCs per cluster, plotted against each other; points above the diagonal separate patients better by embedding than by abundance. Black outline, P < 0.05; numbered, q < 0.05. Because the embedding measure excludes cell counts, separation reflects differences in the appearance of cells assigned to the same population. **c**, Patient-level test AUROC of classifiers trained, like SPECTRA, on the 1,793 training patients (1,689 cancer patients, 104 controls) and scored once on the held-out test cohort. Cluster composition: centred log-ratio shares of each patient's input cells in the 23 clusters (training cells assigned to clusters as test cells were; logistic regression, C = 0.1, class-balanced). Composition + mean cell embedding: the same with the patient's mean embedding (50 principal components) added. SPECTRA: locked model. Right, paired differences on the same patients. 95% CIs and two-sided P from a stratified patient bootstrap (B = 4,000). Clusters were defined on test-cohort cells without labels; no classifier in **c** saw a test label. Exploratory; the smallest attainable permutation P in **a** and **b** is 0.005.

**Supplementary Fig. 6 | What the main high-attention clusters are.** Exploratory: imaging mass cytometry (IMC) and single-cell low-pass (~0.1x) whole-genome sequencing of high-attention cells from the Fig. 3 clusters with the most evidence (numbers and names as in Fig. 3); no statistics. Model emphasis is the log2 relative weight of each cluster among attributed cells (Fig. 3b). **a**, IMC, one column per segmented high-attention cell on a usable-signal slide (cancer patients and non-cancer controls, training and test), grouped by cluster; filled, positive; light, negative; blank, marker not in that slide's panel; orange, cell from a non-cancer control; above the grid, example IMC cells with the cluster's dominant marker(s) (40x centred on the cell, or 10x where marked; bars, 10 µm). Calls as in Supplementary Fig. 7 (counts above off-cell background beyond Poisson noise, at least 3-fold and 0.3 counts per pixel). Cluster 20 had no IMC-linked cells. **b**, Copy-number profiles of every sequenced high-attention cell from held-out test cancer patients in these clusters, one row per cell (log2 segment value relative to the cell's median, autosomes on a common 2-Mb grid), grouped by cluster and by patient (black lines); strips, patient (a distinct colour for each of the 28 patients, the same colour where a patient appears in more than one cluster; no identifiers), cancer type and called altered (dark). Right, three sequenced example cells per cluster from different patients (cluster-majority call, lowest noise); 40x re-images centred on the matched cell (as in Fig. 3: the 40x object whose four-channel profile best matches the cell's 10x profile, near the field centre) or 10x where no 40x exists (marked). All example cells show the same 35 µm field (40x, 0.18 µm per pixel; 10x, 0.59 µm per pixel); bars, 10 µm. A cell is called altered when segments of at least ~10 Mb, shifted by at least 0.2 log2 and at least 6 standard errors beyond that cell's own bin-level noise, cover at least 5% of the autosomes (first-pass caller; ultra-low-pass profiles are noisy, and some short high-frequency changes are noise). On normal-blood-donor cells, 16 of 18 are called flat; one donor sample whose 10 cells share a single clonal profile was excluded as a likely spike-in or mislabelled sample. CK+ clusters (20, 21, 17) are mostly aneuploid, with recurrent patient-specific changes; the vimentin-high cluster 10 (CD31+ or myeloid-marker cells by IMC) and cluster 16 (CD44+, CD45RA+ or CD68+ cells) are mostly copy-number flat. Picked and sequenced cells are a selected sample (chosen at the microscope as candidate tumour cells) and few per cluster, so these data describe cluster biology and do not measure how well SPECTRA detects aneuploid cells.

**Supplementary Fig. 8 | Adaptation methods compared.** Lung cancer versus benign lung nodules and breast cancer versus BRCA carriers without cancer (Fig. 5), each adapted with a fixed recipe on the task's training patients only. Methods: the unchanged pan-cancer model; refitting only the decision layer on the frozen model's patient summary (logistic regression, C = 0.1, class-balanced; used in Fig. 5); retraining the last transformer block together with the decision layer; and low-rank adapters (LoRA; query and value projections, rank 4, alpha 8, all six blocks) trained together with the decision layer. Network adaptations used one fixed budget (5 epochs of 64 updates, AdamW, learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁵), not tuned. **a**, Held-out test AUROC (three-model ensemble) with 95% CIs from a stratified patient bootstrap (B = 4,000); test, 62 lung cases and 23 controls, 218 breast cases and 8 controls. Paired gain over the unchanged model, lung: decision layer 0.10 (−0.04 to 0.24), last block 0.07 (−0.02 to 0.16), LoRA 0.05 (−0.04 to 0.14); breast: 0.35 (0.10 to 0.62), 0.28 (0.10 to 0.46), 0.05 (0.01 to 0.09). **b**, Training-cohort cross-validation (three folds, each adapting the fold's source model; open circles, folds; line, mean). Refitting the decision layer is the simplest method, leaves the cell-reading model unchanged, and performed as well as the methods that retrain part of the network: best on the test set in both tasks, best in breast cross-validation and comparable in lung cross-validation.
